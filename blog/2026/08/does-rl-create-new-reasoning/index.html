Paper collision

Does reinforcement learning create new reasoning abilities?

Higher accuracy may reflect a new ability or a higher probability for an existing answer. Four ICML 2026 papers provide complementary evidence.

Heng ZhangUpdated August 4, 202612 min read
On this page Preface Defining a new ability Reachability at inference Mode contraction in training Method comparison Separating three changes References

Preface

Reinforcement learning is now a common stage in the training of reasoning models. When pass@1 rises on mathematics or code tasks, the result is often described as stronger reasoning. Pass@1 records whether the first sample succeeds. It does not reveal whether the correct trajectory first appeared after training or was already located in a low probability region of the base model.

This distinction changes how a training method should be interpreted. If the correct solution already exists, training may mainly adjust probability ranking. If the solution is present but rarely visible under a finite budget, training may improve empirical reachability. Evidence for ability expansion is stronger only when a new solution structure cannot be recovered from the base model through inference scaling and transfers reliably to new tasks.

This article studies Power Sampling, Verbalized Sampling, SAGE, and G²RPO [1][2][3][4]. Their tasks and algorithms differ, but each asks how much observed capability change can be explained by reorganizing a distribution.

Defining a new ability

Almost every finite token sequence can have nonzero probability under a softmax language model. Theoretical support is therefore not a practical definition of ability. A correct answer with probability close to zero remains invisible under a realistic rollout budget.

A compute dependent reachable set provides a more useful description. The notation below is introduced here to organize the four papers rather than taken from any single paper.

RB(x) = { y | y can be sampled and verified within budget B }

The budget may include the number of samples, maximum generation length, verifier calls, and inference time. Model improvement can then be divided into three levels.

The first level changes the rank of default answers. The second changes the boundary of RB(x). The third additionally requires stability and transfer. The four papers mainly provide evidence about the first two levels.

Track one　Visible capability can change without parameter updates

Power Sampling reorders complete trajectories

Power Sampling modifies the base model distribution over complete responses [1]. When α is greater than 1, higher probability trajectories receive additional mass.

pα(y | x) = p(y | x)α / Z α > 1

The complete trajectory is the important part. Lower temperature only sharpens the next token distribution. If the model locally prefers an early shortcut that later fails, local sharpening can amplify the wrong choice. The power distribution uses future rollouts to correct early decisions.

On Qwen2.5-7B, base pass@1 scores on MATH500, HumanEval, and GPQA are 0.498, 0.329, and 0.278. Power Sampling raises them to 0.708, 0.756, and 0.349. The corresponding GRPO model scores 0.740, 0.561, and 0.354. A substantial part of the observed training gain can therefore be recovered through inference time distribution sharpening.

Verbalized Sampling recovers tail responses

Verbalized Sampling studies a different source of contraction [3]. Preference annotators often favor responses that are familiar, fluent, and typical. When several responses have similar utility, typicality becomes an additional ranking signal and amplifies modes that were already common.

The method does not retrain the model. It asks for several candidates with explicit probabilities and samples from that distribution. Across poetry, stories, jokes, dialogue simulation, and synthetic data, the method increases output diversity without a comparable loss in human rated quality.

Both papers keep the model parameters fixed while changing observed behavior. Power Sampling reshapes probabilities over complete reasoning trajectories. Verbalized Sampling changes how the model expresses and samples a candidate distribution. Default decoding is therefore not a reliable boundary for represented capability.

Track two　Correct modes can compete during training

Reverse KL stability and SAGE

Reverse KL keeps an RLVR policy close to a reference model. The constraint stabilizes training but can make rare correct trajectories harder to sample. SAGE keeps the KL term and reshapes its anchor with a guide function [2].

π*(y | x) ∝ πref(y | x) exp(r / β) π*SAGE(y | x) ∝ q(x, y) πref(y | x) exp(r / β)

The guide function uses signals such as surprisal or entropy to identify reasoning branches and increase the chance of exploring low density correct trajectories. SAGE improves both pass@1 and pass@k at larger sampling budgets on several mathematical tasks.

Support expansion in this paper is empirical support expansion. A trajectory must still have nonzero probability under the reference policy. SAGE changes whether it crosses the visibility threshold under finite rollouts rather than adding theoretical support from nothing.

G²RPO describes winner take all dynamics

G²RPO clusters correct rollouts into solution families and studies the probability flow among them [4]. In its mean field approximation, standard GRPO continually increases concentration among correct modes.

Ly = ||y||22

As this quantity approaches 1, probability mass concentrates on one correct mode. The model may still use different wording and retain high token entropy while relying on one underlying solution strategy.

G²RPO adds an advantage bonus related to the inverse probability of a rare correct mode. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, GRPO reduces the average correct mode count from 2.35 to 1.97. G²RPO raises it to 6.40. AIME 2025 accuracy also rises from 46.7 percent to 48.9 percent.

The mode count is produced by embedding and DBSCAN clustering. Absolute values depend on the encoder and clustering radius, so the result is best interpreted as a relative trend under a fixed proxy.

Method comparison

The four papers do not answer the same experimental question. The following table places them in one framework and separates their intervention, evidence, and limitation.

WorkWhat changesTrainingMain evidenceDoes not directly show
Power SamplingComplete trajectory distributionNoReward free sampling recovers part of the GRPO gainAll RL gains are sharpening
Verbalized SamplingExpression and sampling of candidate distributionsNoDiversity returns across open ended tasksMathematical reasoning must improve
SAGEReference anchor in reverse KLYespass@1 and pass@k improve togetherNew theoretical support appears

Power Sampling and Verbalized Sampling study capability expression at inference. SAGE and G²RPO study capability contraction during training. The first pair shows that behavior can change without parameter updates. The second pair shows that optimizing accuracy can reduce coverage of correct solutions. Together they point to distributional reorganization rather than a simple rejection of reinforcement learning.

Separating probability reordering, reachability, and ability expansion

Comparing base and RL models only at pass@1 cannot separate the three forms of change. A more complete experiment needs at least four steps.

  1. Fix total compute. Use the same token, rollout, and verifier budget for base and RL models.
  2. Plot recovery curves. Compare standard sampling, low temperature sampling, Power Sampling, and the RL model across pass@k budgets.
  3. Inspect solution families. Report semantic clusters, program structure, and key reasoning steps, followed by sensitivity tests for clustering choices.
  4. Test out of distribution transfer. A solution found only in the training distribution is more consistent with probability reordering. Stable cross task transfer gives stronger evidence of expansion.

If stronger sampling lets the base model match the RL model, probability reordering explains the main gain. If RL makes low probability modes reliably visible under a fixed budget while those modes remain recoverable from the base model, improved empirical reachability is the more accurate description. Ability expansion becomes a stronger interpretation only when new solution structures cannot be recovered from the base model and transfer to new task distributions.

Conclusion

The four papers do not support a single binary answer. Accuracy gains after reinforcement learning can contain both distribution sharpening and changes in exploration. Current evidence explains how existing abilities are selected and recovered more clearly than it demonstrates entirely new abilities. Stronger claims require controlled budgets, mode analysis, and out of distribution evaluation.

The useful question is therefore not only how much accuracy increased. It is which solutions already existed, which first became reachable under a fixed budget, and which continued to work beyond the training distribution.
