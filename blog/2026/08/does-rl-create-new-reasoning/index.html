Paper guide

Does reinforcement learning create new reasoning abilities?

When pass@1 rises, has the model learned a new reasoning method, or has an old correct answer become easier to sample? Four ICML 2026 papers offer different pieces of evidence.

Heng ZhangUpdated August 4, 202620 min read
On this page Preface Power Sampling SAGE G²RPO Verbalized Sampling Method summary Returning to the question References

Preface

RLVR papers often begin with an accuracy table. A trained model reaches a higher pass@1 on AIME or MATH and is then described as a stronger reasoner. The difficulty is that pass@1 only tells us that the first sample is more likely to be correct. It does not tell us whether the successful strategy appeared after training or had always occupied a low-probability region of the base model.

Pass@k adds a second view. If pass@1 rises while larger-k performance does not, the model may be repeating a narrower set of high-probability solutions. Conversely, if an inference method recovers a training gain without changing model parameters, default decoding clearly did not expose everything that the model could generate.

This guide reads Power Sampling, SAGE, G²RPO, and Verbalized Sampling [1][2][3][4]. The first three directly study reasoning-time sampling or RLVR training. The last focuses on mode collapse in open-ended generation and serves as broader supporting evidence. The papers do not share one set of assumptions, and they should not be made to deliver one identical conclusion.

[ICML 2026] Scalable Power Sampling

Ji et al.　Training-free sharpening over full trajectories　Math, code, and knowledge QA

The paper starts with a locally attractive mistake. Suppose a model answering 2+2 assigns probability 0.6 to guessing immediately and 0.4 to planning before calculating. Guessing is locally more likely, but the planning branch has a much higher probability of eventually reaching the correct answer. At temperature 0.25, the local preference is amplified and the probability of choosing GUESS rises to roughly 0.835.

The target is a distribution over complete responses. For a base sequence distribution p(y|x), the paper samples from

pα(y | x) = p(y | x)α / Zα > 1

Computing the normalizer directly requires summing over future sequences. Earlier work therefore relied on MCMC and paid a large inference cost. This paper decomposes the global distribution into a low-temperature next-token distribution and a future-aware scaling factor. For each Top-K candidate token, the algorithm draws lookahead rollouts, estimates the likelihood of the resulting continuations, and applies a jackknife correction to remove the leading finite-sample bias. A blockwise implementation avoids rolling all the way to the end at every token.

The experiment supports partial recovery, not equivalence. Power Sampling beats the math-trained GRPO checkpoint on HumanEval but remains below it on MATH500. It also reduces the slowest MCMC setting from 2.5 minutes per problem to 0.22 minutes. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, its duplicate-answer rate across eight rollouts is 62.5 percent, compared with 83.3 percent for GRPO. The base model clearly contains useful trajectories that ordinary decoding does not exploit well. The result does not show that every RL gain is sharpening, nor does it test whether training creates new solution structures.

[ICML 2026] SAGE

Lee, Kang, and Hwang　Reshaping the reverse-KL anchor　Mathematical RLVR

This paper asks why low-probability correct trajectories become difficult to see. Reverse-KL regularization keeps an RLVR policy near a reference model. The anchor reduces reward overfitting, but its stationary solution still weights outcomes multiplicatively by their reference probability. A correct trajectory with very low reference probability may rarely enter a finite rollout batch even after receiving positive reward.

π*(y | x) ∝ πref(y | x) exp(r / β)π*SAGE(y | x) ∝ q(x, y) πref(y | x) exp(r / β)

SAGE keeps the KL constraint but changes its anchor from πref to qπref. The guide q is frozen within each policy update and raises the relative weight of selected low-density regions. The paper tests random perturbations, a token guide based on surprisal, and a branch guide based on entropy. Surprisal often detects unusual wording. Entropy rises when several continuations are locally plausible and is therefore a better lightweight proxy for a reasoning branch.

Empirical support is the key distinction. The paper defines empirical support as reward-valid completions whose probability exceeds a threshold ε. Theorem 4.3 gives a sufficient condition under which a low-probability correct trajectory crosses ε under SAGE. It is an existence result. During training, the method does not know which rare trajectory will be correct, so practical guides can only use intrinsic signals such as entropy to approximate useful branching points.

The branch guide achieves the strongest average pass@1 and pass@256 across the three math datasets. SAGE also produces a stronger pass@k curve on the out-of-distribution Knights and Knaves benchmark. Its theoretical support nevertheless remains fixed. A trajectory with zero reference probability is still unreachable. The paper explicitly presents stronger-teacher distillation as a complementary route that can introduce genuinely new reasoning patterns.

[ICML 2026] G²RPO

Rad et al.　GRPO as a probability flow over reasoning modes　AIME mathematical reasoning

Correct solutions can compete with one another. G²RPO does not treat every token sequence as an independent strategy. It clusters semantically similar correct rollouts into solution families. For one prompt, the policy is then coarse-grained into a distribution over good and bad modes. Under a KL-free, binary-verifier, mean-field abstraction, standard GRPO amplifies small differences among the good modes.

Ly = ||y||221 / K ≤ Ly ≤ 1

With K equally weighted correct modes, Ly is near 1/K. With all mass on one mode, it is one. The paper shows that, absent exact ties, the GRPO collision field makes the largest coordinate increasingly dominant and converges toward a winner-take-all state. This is more specific than token entropy because several differently worded answers can still implement the same reasoning plan.

The implementation only modifies rollout advantages. A group of 16 rollouts is split into correct and incorrect samples. Correct samples are embedded and clustered with DBSCAN. A correct rollout in a low-frequency cluster receives an inverse-probability bonus. A good-only bonus can slow the removal of bad mass, so a neutrality correction keeps the error-reduction channel close to standard GRPO.

Both backbones show the same direction. GRPO raises accuracy but pushes average correct-mode coverage below the base model. G²RPO raises both. The claim still has a clear boundary. The theory is mean-field, the main experiments are AIME-centered, and embedding clusters are not an intrinsic definition of reasoning. Only modes observed in a finite rollout group can receive a bonus, so very long-tail correct strategies may remain invisible.

[ICML 2026] Verbalized Sampling

Zhang et al.　Recovering aligned-model diversity with distribution prompts　Open-ended generation and synthetic data

This paper studies a different form of mode collapse. The authors identify typicality bias in preference data. When true quality is similar, annotators are more likely to prefer a familiar, fluent response with higher base-model probability. In the optimum of KL-regularized RLHF, this preference behaves like an additional power sharpening of the reference distribution. Common responses become even more common.

Verbalized Sampling changes no training algorithm. It changes the semantic object requested by the prompt. A conventional prompt asks for one answer, whose typical output is often one prototypical instance. VS asks for several candidates and their probabilities, making a representative distribution the typical object. The authors also compare against list prompts. Asking for a list alone still tends to return a bestseller list of common answers. Explicit distribution elicitation is the central difference.

VS improves the diversity-quality frontier in creative writing, dialogue simulation, open-ended QA, and synthetic data. Larger models usually benefit more. It also recovers part of the diversity lost across Tulu's SFT, DPO, and RLVR stages. Most of these experiments are not mathematical reasoning. The paper shows that aligned models retain generative variation hidden by default prompts. It does not directly show whether mathematical RLVR creates a new reasoning method. It is supporting evidence rather than an equal fourth pillar of the RLVR argument.

Method summary

The four papers can be connected through distributional change, but they intervene at different locations and support claims of different strength.

Power Sampling shows that full-trajectory inference can change accuracy without parameter updates. SAGE shows that reverse-KL stability makes low-density correct trajectories difficult to sample during finite training. G²RPO goes further and argues that correct solution families themselves can enter winner-take-all competition. VS shows a related contraction in open-ended generation. Together they reject the idea that default output is the boundary of model capability. They do not jointly prove that RL merely reorders old answers.

Returning to the original question

After reading the four papers, it is useful to separate three increasingly strong meanings of new ability. This framework belongs after the evidence rather than before it.

The strongest evidence in these papers concerns the first two levels. Supporting the third requires matched token, rollout, and verifier budgets, full pass@k recovery curves for both base and RL models, and solution-family analysis based on program structure or decisive reasoning steps. Out-of-distribution transfer is also necessary to distinguish a general capability from sharpening inside the training distribution.

Assessment
Higher RLVR accuracy should not be attributed automatically to new ability, but it should not be reduced automatically to distribution sharpening either. Current evidence is much stronger on how correct trajectories are hidden, discovered, and compressed than on whether genuinely new reasoning structures appear.
